# Interview Talking Points

Read the Job, Posting, Match, relevant Career Profile and documents, authorized Notes, Company research and existing Talking Points. Use status and Notes for application context, not as proof of company facts.

Prepare points for this role, company and known interview stage, adapting their number and depth to the request. Roughly six to eight points is a useful starting point. Titles can be likely interviewer questions or specific topics the person wants to raise; include both where useful. Cover relevant technical or product judgment, collaboration and behavioral evidence, and label assumptions about interviewer intent.

Before writing an affected Response, ask the person to clarify missing or conflicting facts that would materially change the answer. A missing Career Profile entry establishes neither experience nor inexperience. Keep that Response undrafted and unsaved until the facts are resolved; do not substitute a conditional or evasive script for clarification. The person's account can establish the facts without formal proof. Clarification alone does not authorize a Career Profile update.

Once the facts are established, write each Response as a developed first-person answer in Markdown bullets, ready to say or read aloud. Answer the question directly in complete, natural sentences that develop context, actions or proposed approach, reasoning, supported outcomes and role relevance. Three to five bullets is often enough, but adapt to the answer and field limits. A Response must be a complete answer, not memory cues or coaching such as “mention your experience.”

Keep preparation notes, objective company facts, difficult follow-ups and open questions outside the spoken response. Frame transferable experience and substantive gaps honestly, and distinguish future approaches from past experience. Propose ordered points with Low, Medium or High Priority and explain their relevance. Preserve useful existing points during focused changes.

Check the drafted Response for factual accuracy and read it as spoken words. Make sure it answers the question naturally in the person's voice, without commentary about checking evidence or preparing the answer. Then follow the shared review and save guidance.
